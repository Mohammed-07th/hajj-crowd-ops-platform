# SOP-MED-002 — Medical Emergency Response

## 1. Purpose

Defines triage categories, the P1 medical protocol, and the conditions under
which an ambulance corridor is activated through a dense crowd.

Response and resolution targets are defined in **SOP-OPS-001**, not here. A P1
medical request carries a **4-minute response target and a 30-minute resolution
target**.

## 2. P1 medical definition

A request is P1 MEDICAL only where one or more of the following is present:

- Unconsciousness or altered consciousness
- Absent, obstructed or severely laboured breathing
- Chest pain with suspected cardiac origin
- Uncontrolled haemorrhage
- Suspected heat stroke — core temperature indication with altered mental state
  (see **SOP-MED-009**)
- Crush or trampling injury

Everything else is P2 or lower. The narrow definition is deliberate: a 4-minute
target is only achievable if P1 volume stays low.

## 3. Triage categories

| Category | Colour | Meaning | Target to definitive care |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Red | Immediate — life threat, treatable | 15 minutes |
| T2 | Yellow | Urgent — serious, can wait briefly | 60 minutes |
| T3 | Green | Delayed — walking wounded | 240 minutes |
| T4 | Black | Expectant / deceased | Per Civil Defence direction |

Triage is performed by the first qualified responder on scene and re-performed
on arrival at the medical point. A category may be raised at any time; lowering
a category requires a physician.

## 4. P1 protocol

1. **Acknowledge within 4 minutes.** Controller assigns a crew; the request
   moves to `DISPATCHED` with a crew identifier attached. A dispatched request
   without a crew identifier is invalid.
2. **Nearest-resource dispatch.** The nearest crew responds regardless of sector
   boundary. Sector ownership is reconciled afterwards, never during.
3. **Triage on arrival**, category recorded against the request.
4. **Stabilise in place** where movement carries risk. In high-density zones
   moving a patient can be more dangerous than treating where they lie.
5. **Decide extraction route** — stretcher route or ambulance corridor
   (section 5).
6. **Handover at the medical point** with triage category, time of onset and
   interventions given.
7. **Resolve** the request only once the patient is under the care of the
   receiving medical point.

## 5. Ambulance corridor activation

An ambulance corridor is a temporarily cleared lane through a crowd. It is
disruptive by design and creates counterflow, so it is authorised, not assumed.

**Activation conditions — all three must hold:**

1. A T1 (red) patient, or two or more T2 patients from the same incident.
2. No viable stretcher route to a medical point within **8 minutes**.
3. Zone utilisation permits a lane to be cleared without pushing an adjacent
   zone above 90% (cross-check against **SOP-CS-004** thresholds).

**Authorisation:** the **SOC Controller** activates a corridor, consistent with
the diversion authority in **SOP-CS-011**. Where corridor activation requires
diverting crowd flow out of a zone at or above 95% utilisation, the **Duty
Operations Director** must authorise, because that is a diversion under
SOP-CS-011 section 3.

**Execution:**
- Marshals form the lane from the destination end backwards toward the patient.
- Minimum clear width **2.5 metres**.
- Maximum corridor hold time **6 minutes**; beyond this, re-evaluate, because a
  held corridor is itself accumulating density at its edges.
- Wayfinding staff manage the displaced flow; the corridor is closed lane by
  lane as the ambulance passes, never all at once.

## 6. Medical points

Each site maintains staffed medical points with the capability to stabilise T1
patients. Crews report point status at the start of every shift under
**SOP-OPS-006**. A medical point that cannot accept T1 patients must be reported
immediately under **SOP-OPS-020**, because it changes every extraction decision
in its catchment.

## 7. Heat-related presentations

Heat exhaustion and heat stroke rise sharply with the WBGT index. Thresholds,
cooling activation and the relationship between heat and crowd density are in
**SOP-MED-009**. Where WBGT is above the SOP-MED-009 cooling threshold, medical
crews pre-position at the highest-density zones rather than waiting for calls.
